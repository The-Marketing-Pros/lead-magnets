Phase 1 — Pre-Application Preparation
Get organized before you submit a single form. Most rejections start here.
0/8
›
Collect all provider documents in one folder
DEA certificate, medical license, board certs, malpractice certs, CV, NPI confirmation, diploma, internship/residency letters
First
Verify NPI is active and taxonomy codes are correct
Check NPPES registry at npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov. Wrong taxonomy = wrong reimbursement rates.
Create or update CAQH ProView profile
Most commercial payers pull from CAQH. Incomplete profiles cause automatic delays. Attest within last 90 days.
Critical
Confirm malpractice coverage is current and adequate
Most payers require minimum $1M/$3M. Get a certificate of insurance naming the practice entity.
Verify DEA certificate is active and matches practice address
DEA address must match the practice location on each application. Mismatches cause rejection.
Critical
Confirm state license is active, unrestricted, and matches application state
Run a license verification lookup. Even good-standing licenses can have administrative holds.
Identify target payers and prioritize by patient volume
Don't apply everywhere at once. Rank payers by expected volume. Start with Medicare and your top 3 commercial payers.
Set up a credentialing tracking spreadsheet or tool
Track: payer name, date submitted, contact, status, follow-up dates, effective date. Use our free template (Lead Magnet #4).

Phase 2 — Application Submission
Accurate first submissions are 3x faster to approve. Errors here cost weeks.
0/9
›
Submit Medicare enrollment via PECOS (if not already enrolled)
PECOS is the CMS enrollment system. New providers: allow 60–90 days. Reassignment of benefits must match billing arrangement.
Priority
Submit Medicaid enrollment (state-specific portal)
Each state has its own Medicaid portal. Check state-specific requirements; some require a separate rendering provider enrollment.
Apply with BCBS (local plan — not national)
BlueCross plans are regionally managed. Apply to the specific local plan for each state/market you serve.
Apply with Aetna
Submit via Aetna's provider portal or NaviNet. Aetna often has a 90–120 day timeline in competitive markets.
Apply with UnitedHealthcare
UHC requires UHCprovider.com registration. Allow 90+ days. Follow up every 3 weeks minimum.
Apply with Cigna and other specialty payers for your market
Include any payers that represent >5% of your expected patient volume.
Double-check all addresses are consistent across applications
Practice address, billing address, NPI address, CAQH address must all match. A single mismatch can cause a hold.
Don't skip
Confirm correct billing NPI vs. rendering NPI on each application
Group NPI (Type 2) for billing entity; individual NPI (Type 1) for the rendering provider. Mixing these causes claim rejections.
Don't skip
Record submission date, confirmation number, and contact for each application
You will need this when you follow up. No confirmation number = no proof of submission.

Phase 3 — Active Follow-Up
Applications don't move on their own. Systematic follow-up is what cuts timelines.
0/7
›
Follow up with every payer 2–3 weeks after submission
Call or use portal. Verify application is in queue, no missing documents. Get the name of your rep if possible.
Set calendar reminders to follow up every 3 weeks until approved
Consistency beats urgency. Payers prioritize applications that stay in front of them.
Leverage
Respond to any additional information requests within 48 hours
Every day of delay on your end resets your position in the queue. Treat payer AIRs as fire drills.
Time-sensitive
Log every follow-up call: date, rep name, status, next step
This creates a paper trail if you need to escalate. "I spoke to Maria on June 3rd" is more powerful than "we called them."
Confirm CAQH attestation hasn't expired during the wait
CAQH attestations expire every 120 days. An expired CAQH during a pending application can restart the clock.
Easy miss
Check for any license or DEA renewals due during the credentialing window
A license that expires while your application is pending will cause an automatic hold.
Escalate stalled applications (>90 days without status change)
Ask for a supervisor. Reference your submission date and confirmation number. Escalations often break logjams.
✅
Phase 4 — Approval & Go-Live
Approval isn't go-live. These steps turn an approval into billable claims.
0/6
›
Obtain written confirmation of effective date from each payer
The effective date determines when you can start billing. Don't assume — get it in writing.
Don't skip
Add provider to your practice management / billing system
Update rendering provider ID, group contract numbers, and fee schedule for this payer in your PM system.
Verify EDI/ERA enrollment is active for electronic remittance
Without EDI/ERA setup, you'll get paper EOBs and manual posting. This adds cost and delay to your revenue cycle.
Submit a test claim and verify it processes correctly
One test claim per payer on day one of go-live. Watch for rejection codes — they surface setup errors before they become volume problems.
Pro move
Confirm fee schedule rates match your contract
Run 5–10 EOBs through your contract rate vs. paid-rate checker. Underpayments are common in the first 90 days.
Update provider directory listing with payer
Patients searching in-network find your provider via payer directories. This often lags approval by 30–60 days — follow up proactively.
🔄
Phase 5 — Ongoing Maintenance
Re-credentialing failures are a hidden revenue killer. Automate these or they will get missed.
0/6
›
Set re-credentialing reminders for every payer (typically every 2–3 years)
Set reminders 90 days before each payer's re-credentialing deadline. Pull the dates from your approval letters now.
Automate
Set CAQH re-attestation reminder every 90 days
Put it in your calendar now. CAQH expiration during a re-credentialing cycle is a top-5 cause of preventable payment holds.
Top 5 miss
Track license and DEA expiration dates on a rolling 12-month calendar
Renew 60 days before expiration. Some state boards have backlogs — don't wait until 30 days out.
Review payer contracts annually for rate changes or term updates
Payers send contract amendments via letter. Many practices never see them. Add a rate audit to your annual revenue cycle review.
Update payer directories when provider information changes
Address change, phone, accepting new patients status. Out-of-date directory listings cost you referrals and cause patient friction.
Audit underpayments quarterly against contracted rates
Industry studies show 3–5% of paid claims are underpaid. On $2M in collections, that's $60–100k/year sitting uncollected.
